## FAQ

- **Q: What's the difference between ECC and non-ECC RAM?**
  A: ECC (Error-Correcting Code) memory detects and corrects single-bit errors automatically, preventing silent data corruption. Non-ECC consumer RAM can leave single-bit errors uncorrected, which in a long render or database operation can corrupt data. For professional workloads, ECC is the standard.

- **Q: RDIMM vs UDIMM — can I mix them?**
  A: No — RDIMM (Registered) and UDIMM (Unbuffered) are physically and electrically incompatible. Threadripper 7000/9000 workstations require RDIMM. Consumer AM5 desktops use UDIMM.

- **Q: How much RAM can a Threadripper 7000 / 9000 workstation hold?**
  A: Up to 768GB (12 slots × 64GB RDIMM) or 384GB (8 slots × 48GB) depending on the board. This 32GB module is the entry unit; scale up with additional modules.

- **Q: Is 5600 MT/s slow for a workstation?**
  A: 5600 is the JEDEC DDR5 baseline speed, validated for Threadripper 7000/9000. For most professional workloads (CAD, rendering, data science), 5600 is sufficient. The 6400 MT/s module (KSM64R52BD4-64HA) is the step up for maximum bandwidth.
