## FAQ

**Q: 250W TDP — is that enough for my CPU?** — 250W covers Ryzen 5 7600X (105W boost), Ryzen 7 9700X (105W boost), Core i5-14600K (181W boost), and Core i7-14700K (253W boost, borderline). For Ryzen 9 9950X or heavy overclocking, a higher-tier AIO like the Frozen Warframe Pro or Peerless Vision is recommended.

**Q: How does the Frozen Infinity compare to Thermalright's air coolers?** — The Frozen Infinity is in the AIO segment, while Thermalright's Phantom Spirit 120 and Peerless Assassin 120 are air coolers. For CPUs under 180W, a high-end air cooler can match the Frozen Infinity's thermals at a lower price. The 360mm AIO advantage appears with higher-TDP CPUs, in NZ summer heat, and for builders who prefer the aesthetics of liquid cooling.

**Q: Is the Frozen Infinity above or below the Aqua Elite?** — The Frozen Infinity sits above the Aqua Elite in Thermalright's AIO hierarchy. The Aqua Elite is the budget entry line; the Frozen Infinity is the budget-to-mid-tier step-up with higher TDP (250W vs 220W), faster pump (2800 RPM vs 2600 RPM), and higher fan speeds (1800 RPM vs 1600 RPM).

**Q: What's the noise level like?** — The fans reach 27 dBA at maximum speed (1800 RPM). At idle (500 RPM), the fans are near-silent. The pump operates at a low audible level. For a gaming case with case fans and a GPU fan running, the Frozen Infinity's noise is well-masked during typical use.
