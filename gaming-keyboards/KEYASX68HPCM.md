## Selling Points

- **Magnetic switches with rapid trigger — the competitive feature, at a mid-tier price** — a traditional mechanical key has to travel back past its reset point before it can register again. With rapid trigger, the key re-actuates after as little as 0.1mm of movement in the opposite direction. In CS2 and Valorant that's faster counter-strafing and more consistent bunny-hopping — the single biggest input-feel advantage a keyboard can give you.

- **No contact wear — the longevity argument for magnetic** — Hall Effect sensing reads the magnet's position instead of closing a metal contact, so there's no contact-oxidation ceiling of the kind mechanical switches carry (industry rating: 50–100 million keystrokes). On a board you intend to keep for years, the wear mechanism simply isn't there.

- **65% layout for the smallest practical footprint** — 66 keys drops the F-row and the dedicated arrow cluster, which is the trade that frees the most desk width for the mouse. For a small desk, a dual-monitor arrangement, or a board that travels to LAN events, that's the size that fits.

- **Pink Contour is a designed case, not a recolor** — the contoured finish reads as intentional from the desk angle and breaks away from the black-and-white default of the magnetic segment. It suits pastel-toned setups and stands out in a bag of identical peripherals.

- **Wired USB-C keeps the input chain as short as possible** — no battery, no dongle, nothing to check before a ranked session. At this tier that's the honest configuration for a board that lives on one desk.

## FAQ

- **Q: Does it support rapid trigger?**
  A: The board uses magnetic switches, which is the technology that enables rapid trigger and adjustable actuation. If rapid trigger is a hard requirement for your play, confirm the current firmware feature set with the Build Team before purchase — they can check the live spec at Onehunga.

- **Q: Why no arrow keys or F-row?**
  A: The 65% layout puts those on the Fn layer by design, which is the trade for the smallest possible footprint and maximum mouse space. For spreadsheet, IDE, or DAW work, a 75% or TKL is the better fit.

- **Q: Is the magnetic feel different from a mechanical switch?**
  A: Yes, in two ways. The actuation point is adjustable in software rather than fixed by the switch, and HE switches typically need a heavier return spring to overcome the magnet-sensor interaction — so a "light" HE switch can feel heavier on the return than a comparably-rated mechanical linear. For gaming the faster reset is the payoff.

- **Q: Why is it wired only?**
  A: At this tier, wired keeps the price down and removes the battery and dongle variables entirely. If you need wireless, the Attack Shark X85 Pro is the 2.4GHz option in the same range.

- **Q: How much stock is there?**

- **Q: Can I use it with a Mac?**
  A: USB-C wired works on macOS out of the box. The actuation and rapid-trigger software is Windows-first, so on Mac you'd be using the system defaults.
