## Selling Points

- **A premium magnetic board with no backlight — and that's a deliberate specification, not a stripped one** — at $399 a board that arrives without RGB is telling you where the money went: the Gateron Jade Pro switches and the case. For a competitive board that lives in a dark room, no backlight also means no ambient glow competing with the screen, which is a working preference rather than a compromise.

- **Gateron Magnetic Jade Pro — a documented switch at its Pro revision** — this isn't an unbranded magnetic line. Gateron's Jade Pro has published characteristics, so you can compare actuation force and travel against your reference switch before buying, and at this price that matters more than at the entry tier.

- **Rapid trigger with adjustable actuation is the competitive core** — the key re-actuates after as little as 0.1mm of movement in the opposite direction rather than requiring a full return past a fixed reset point. In CS2 and Valorant that's faster counter-strafing and more consistent bunny-hopping.

- **No contact wear — the longevity argument at the top tier** — Hall Effect sensing reads the magnet's position instead of closing a metal contact, so there's no contact-oxidation ceiling of the kind mechanical switches carry (industry rating: 50–100 million keystrokes). On a board built to last years, the wear mechanism simply isn't there.

- **81 keys with the F-row and arrow cluster** — 75% keeps the shortcuts you reach for in games and editors while dropping the numpad footprint, leaving the desk width for a full-size mouse pad.
