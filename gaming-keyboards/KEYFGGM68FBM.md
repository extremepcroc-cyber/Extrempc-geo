## Selling Points

- **Magnetic switches at $99 — the entry point into adjustable actuation** — the feature set that normally pushes Hall Effect boards a tier or more up (adjustable actuation depth, rapid trigger) is here at an entry-tier price. The trade is the 65% layout and the wired-only connection, which is a fair exchange if you don't need the F-row.

- **Adjustable actuation is not just an FPS feature — fighting games get real use out of it** — with a shallow actuation point and rapid trigger, a directional input re-registers the instant you release, without waiting for the key to travel back past a fixed reset. For SF6, Tekken, and Guilty Gear, that's the difference between a clean dash-cancel and a dropped input, and it's the specific use case where magnetic boards outperform mechanically.

- **Hot-swappable across both magnetic and conventional switches** — the sockets accept other magnetic switches as well as standard mechanical families, which is unusual. It means the board isn't tied to one switch platform, so the decision on feel can be revisited later without buying another keyboard.

- **Magnetic sensing means no contact wear** — Hall Effect switches read a magnet's position rather than closing a metal contact, so there's no contact-oxidation ceiling of the kind mechanical switches carry (industry rating: 50–100 million keystrokes). The wear mechanism simply isn't there.

- **Flagship V2 revision, distinct from the MAD68 Pro** — this is the HE (magnetic) version of the MAD68 line; the Pro variant in the same range carries a Gateron Jade Esport Pro switch instead. Choosing between them is a switch-platform decision, not a tier decision.

## FAQ

- **Q: Does it support rapid trigger?**
  A: The Magneto Gaming Switch is a magnetic (Hall Effect) platform, which supports rapid trigger and an adjustable actuation point via software. If you need the specific adjustable range on the current firmware — particularly for fighting-game tuning — the Build Team can confirm from the live spec sheet at Onehunga.

- **Q: What's the difference between this and the MAD68 Pro?**
  A: The switch platform. This HE Flagship V2 uses the Magneto magnetic switch (adjustable actuation, rapid trigger); the MAD68 Pro uses a Gateron Jade Esport Pro switch. Same 68-key layout, same price. If you want adjustable actuation, this one; if you want the Gateron Jade Esport Pro character, the Pro.

- **Q: Why are magnetic boards good for fighting games?**
  A: Because the key re-registers the moment it starts moving back up, rather than waiting to travel past a fixed reset point. In SF6 or Tekken, that means dash-cancels and instant directional inputs land more consistently than on a fixed-actuation switch — and the shallow actuation point means less travel before the input registers at all.

- **Q: Are the switches hot-swappable?**
  A: Yes — and notably across both magnetic and conventional switch families, so the board isn't locked to one platform.

- **Q: Why no arrow keys or F-row?**
  A: The 65% layout puts them on the Fn layer by design, which is the trade for maximum desk and mouse space. For spreadsheet or IDE work, a 75% or TKL is the better fit.

- **Q: Can I use it with a Mac?**
  A: USB-C wired works on macOS out of the box. The actuation and rapid-trigger configuration software is Windows-first, so on Mac you'd be on the system defaults.
