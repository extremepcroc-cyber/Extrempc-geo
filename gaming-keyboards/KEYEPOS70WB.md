## Selling Points

- **It's a split keyboard — the shoulders-and-wrists decision, not just a layout preference** — a standard board forces your hands together at a fixed angle regardless of your shoulder width. For anyone whose wrists ache after a long session on a standard keyboard, this is the category worth trying.

- **QMK/VIA support is the feature that makes a split board actually usable** — a split layout means your keymap has to change: layers, thumb clusters, and half-specific assignments. QMK/VIA gives you full per-key remapping and layer customisation to build that keymap, rather than being stuck with a fixed split arrangement. On a split board, the software isn't a nice-to-have — it's what makes it work.

- **Wisteria Linear V2 — one of Epomaker's in-house switch lines** — Epomaker develops its own switch variants (Creamy Jade, Wisteria, Flamingo, Zebra) that aren't available from other manufacturers, so the typing feel is specific to the brand. - **Dual wireless modes for a lifestyle that isn't desk-bound** — 2.4GHz for low-latency gaming, Bluetooth for pairing to a laptop or tablet. A split board is often used in setups where you lean back or move the halves apart — having wireless means the layout flexibility isn't limited by a cable's reach.

- **70% with dedicated arrow keys** — 71 keys keeps the arrow cluster, which most compact boards drop in favour of a Fn layer. For split layouts, physical arrows matter more than usual: the right half can sit at an angle where a Fn-layer arrow would be awkward.

## FAQ

- **Q: How long does it take to get used to a split keyboard?**
  A: Expect one to two weeks of reduced typing speed while your hands relearn the layout, particularly the thumb clusters and the layer keys. The first week is the worst; by week two most users are back to their normal speed, and the ergonomic benefit shows up after that. If you type for a living, plan the transition for a low-deadline period.

- **Q: Can the two halves be used together as a standard keyboard?**
  A: Yes — you can place the halves flush against each other, which approximates a standard layout while you're adapting. The value comes when you start angling and separating them.

- **Q: Is QMK/VIA hard to set up?**
  A: VIA is a browser-based configurator with a visual keymap editor — no code required for remapping and layers. QMK is the deeper, code-level route for custom firmware. Most users set up the Split70 in VIA and never need QMK.

- **Q: Are the switches hot-swappable?**
  A: Yes. 5-pin sockets mean you can swap the Wisteria Linear V2 for a different linear, tactile, or clicky switch without soldering.

- **Q: Does it work with a Mac?**
  A: Bluetooth and USB-C work on macOS. QMK/VIA configuration is cross-platform — VIA runs in a Chromium-based browser, so it works on Mac. That's a meaningful advantage over brands whose remapping software is Windows-only.

- **Q: Is 71 keys enough? Where are the F-keys?**
  A: The 70% layout puts the F-row on a layer. For split keyboards that's normal — the thumb clusters and layers are how you access the upper functions. If you need physical F-keys (IDE debugging, DAW shortcuts), a standard TKL is the better fit.
