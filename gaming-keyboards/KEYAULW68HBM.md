## Selling Points

- **Hall Effect magnetic switches at an entry-tier price** — most magnetic (HE) keyboards in the NZ market sit a tier or more above this price point. The WIN68 HE puts AULA's Greywood magnetic switches into the entry tier, which is the main reason to choose it over a same-price mechanical board: adjustable actuation and rapid trigger are features that on other brands mark the price up significantly.

- **65% layout — the most compact option in AULA's range** — 68 keys drops the F-row and the arrow cluster, freeing maximum desk width. For a small desk, a dual-monitor setup where the keyboard sits between the screens, or a travel-ready rig, the 65% footprint is the difference between the board fitting and it crowding the desk.

- **Magnetic switches mean no contact wear** — Hall Effect sensing reads the magnet's position instead of closing a metal contact, so there's no contact-oxidation ceiling the way mechanical switches carry (industry rating: 50–100 million keystrokes). For a board you intend to keep for years, the wear mechanism simply isn't there.

- **Wired keeps the input chain as simple as possible** — at this tier, wired removes the battery and dongle variables entirely. For a fixed competitive battlestation, the USB-C wired connection is the lowest-friction setup there is.

- **Hot-swappable despite the magnetic platform** — the sockets accept other switch families, so if you later want a different feel, the modding door stays open rather than being locked to one switch type.

## FAQ

- **Q: Does it really support rapid trigger, or is that just the HE-series marketing?**
  A: AULA's HE series spec includes software-adjustable actuation and rapid trigger. The exact feature set on the WIN68 HE's current software build is worth confirming with the Build Team before purchase if rapid trigger is a hard requirement — they can verify against the current spec sheet.

- **Q: Why no F-row? Will I lose keyboard shortcuts I use?**
  A: The 65% layout has no dedicated F1–F12 row — F-key functions are accessed via the Fn layer. If your daily workflow (CAD, IDE, DAW) relies on physical F-keys, choose a 75% board like the AULA F75 instead. For gaming and general typing, the Fn layer covers the functions.

- **Q: Is the magnetic feel noticeably different from a mechanical switch?**
  A: Yes. HE switches typically need a heavier return spring to overcome the magnet-sensor interaction, so a "light" HE switch can feel heavier on the return than a comparably-rated mechanical linear. For long typing sessions that's a real consideration; for gaming the faster reset is the payoff.

- **Q: Can I use it with a Mac?**
  A: USB-C wired works on macOS out of the box. The actuation/rapid-trigger software is Windows-first; on Mac you'll use the system defaults.

- **Q: How loud is it?**
  A: Magnetic switches are generally quieter than dry mechanicals on the press because the actuation is sensing-based rather than contact-based, but key sound (keycaps hitting the top case) is the same as any 65% board. A desk mat helps.
