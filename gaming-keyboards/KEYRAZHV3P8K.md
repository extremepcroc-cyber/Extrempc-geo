## Selling Points

- **Analog optical switches — actuation by light beam, not by metal contact** — where a mechanical switch closes a physical contact, the Huntsman V3 Pro reads a beam of light. That removes the metal-on-metal wear mechanism entirely, and Razer rates the Gen-4 optical switch at 100 million clicks. It's a different technology from magnetic Hall Effect boards — the actuation is optical, not magnetic — but it delivers the same practical benefit: adjustable actuation and rapid trigger.

- **Adjustable actuation from 0.1mm to 3.5mm, per key** — you can set a shallow trigger for movement keys in an FPS and a deeper trigger for typing keys on the same board. That's the feature competitive players pay premium prices for, and it's configurable per key rather than globally.

## FAQ

- **Q: Is this a magnetic (Hall Effect) keyboard?**
  A: No — it uses Razer's Analog Optical switches, which read a beam of light rather than a magnetic field. The practical capabilities overlap heavily (adjustable actuation, rapid trigger, no contact wear), but it's a different underlying technology. If you specifically want magnetic switches, the Varmilo Victory/Muse65 or the MCHOSE magnetic boards are the alternatives.

- **Q: What does the 8KHz polling rate actually do?**
  A: It reports key states 8,000 times a second — a 0.125ms interval — versus 1,000Hz on a standard gaming keyboard. Whether that translates to a perceptible advantage is debated, but it's the highest polling tier available and part of what a flagship competitive board buys.

- **Q: How does rapid trigger work on an optical switch?**
  A: The switch reads position continuously rather than only detecting a closed contact, so the key can re-actuate after a small movement in the opposite direction instead of a full return past a fixed reset point. That's the feature that matters for counter-strafing and bunny-hopping in CS2 and Valorant.

- **Q: Is there a wireless version?**
  A: This model is wired USB-C. Wired is standard for flagship competitive boards because it removes the wireless variable entirely.

- **Q: How much stock is there?**
  A: Low stock at the moment — a single unit at Onehunga. Confirm availability with the store before planning around it.

- **Q: What software do I need?**
  A: Razer Synapse handles per-key actuation tuning, rapid trigger configuration, and Chroma lighting. It's Windows-first; check current macOS support if you're on a Mac and need the tuning features.
