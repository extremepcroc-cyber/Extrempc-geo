## Selling Points

- **TTC Dragon Lord magnetic switches — a TTC switch line in a compact board** — MCHOSE sources magnetic switches from several vendors (Kailh, TTC, Apollo, Mount Tai), and the Ace 68 carries TTC's Dragon Lord. TTC is an established switch manufacturer, so this is a known vendor's magnetic line rather than an unbranded one.

- **65% layout for the smallest practical footprint** — 68 keys drops the F-row and the dedicated arrow cluster, freeing the desk width that a bigger board commits. For a small desk, a dual-monitor setup, or a bag that goes to LAN events, the 65% is the size that fits.

- **Rapid trigger is the reason to go magnetic** — a key re-actuates after as little as 0.1mm of movement in the opposite direction, rather than requiring a full return past a fixed reset point. In CS2 and Valorant that's faster counter-strafing and more consistent bunny-hopping — the single biggest input-feel advantage a keyboard can give you.

- **Magnetic switches have no contact wear** — Hall Effect sensing reads the magnet's position instead of closing a metal contact, so there's no contact-oxidation ceiling of the kind mechanical switches carry (industry rating: 50–100 million keystrokes).

- **The topographic black finish is understated but not plain** — a black board with topographic detailing reads as intentional from the desk angle, without committing to a bright colourway. Suits dark setups where the RGB is the accent.
