## FAQ

- **Q: Are red switches good for typing, or only for gaming?**
  A: Both — red linears are light and smooth, which makes them easy on the fingers over a long day and quiet by mechanical standards. If you prefer a tactile bump or a click for typing confirmation, the brown-switch version of this same board (KEYTHUK63BB) is the alternative.

- **Q: How loud is it?**
  A: Red linears have no click and no tactile bump, so they're the quietest of the common mechanical types — but keycap impact on the case is still audible. A desk mat helps. If silence is the hard requirement, a membrane board or a dampened switch set is the better fit.

- **Q: Does it have arrow keys?**
  A: Not physically. The 60% layout puts the arrow cluster and F-row on the Fn layer. If you navigate with arrows constantly, a 65% or 75% board is the better fit.

- **Q: Is it hot-swappable?**
  A: Yes — the sockets accept the standard switch families, so the red linears can be changed for a tactile or clicky set without soldering. That's the reason this board outlives one switch preference.

- **Q: Is it wireless?**
  A: No — wired USB-C. At this tier, wired keeps the price down and removes the battery and dongle variables. For wireless, the Attack Shark X85 Pro or AULA F75 MAX are the options in the range.

- **Q: Can I use it with a Mac?**
  A: USB-C wired works on macOS out of the box. Key remapping software is Windows-first, so on Mac you'd be on the system defaults.
