## Selling Points

- **It's a second device, not a smaller keyboard** — the Tartarus sits beside your keyboard and gives your left hand a dedicated control cluster: 18 macro keys plus a thumb-controlled directional cluster, with the keyboard still available for typing. That's the point — you're not replacing anything, you're adding an input surface that keeps your left hand out of the WASD position's limitations.

- **The 10 directional keys under the thumb are the actual feature** — with a normal keyboard you get four directions under your fingers and nothing under your thumb. The Tartarus puts an 8-way directional cluster plus additional keys under the thumb, so movement, abilities, and camera control can be split between the thumb and the fingers instead of competing for the same keys.

- **Mecha-membrane is the deliberate middle ground** — a mid-height key with a tactile response like a mechanical switch and the softer bottom-out of a membrane. It keeps the keypad quiet relative to a mechanical board, which matters if the keypad sits next to an open microphone.

- **Macro capacity for MMO and complex-input games** — 18 macro keys with full remapping in Razer Synapse. For MMO rotations, flight sims, and games with large ability bars, a spreadsheet-style macro cluster is a genuinely different way to play compared to stretching across a keyboard.

- **Chroma integration with the rest of the setup** — the keypad participates in Razer's 20-million-colour ecosystem with reactive in-game lighting, so it doesn't sit outside the setup's lighting scheme.

## FAQ

- **Q: Is this a keyboard?**
  A: No — it's a single-hand gaming keypad designed to sit alongside your keyboard. You keep typing on the keyboard; the Tartarus gives your left hand a dedicated macro and movement surface.

- **Q: Do I need a Razer keyboard for it to work?**
  A: No. It connects over USB as a standalone device and works with any keyboard. Razer Synapse is needed for remapping and macros, and Chroma synchronisation requires other Chroma-capable devices if you want the lighting to match.

- **Q: Is it good for FPS, or mainly MMO?**
  A: It's most associated with MMO and sim play, where the macro count matters. For FPS, most players prefer a keyboard's WASD position plus mouse — but the thumb cluster can be useful for ability-heavy shooters.

- **Q: Are the switches hot-swappable?**
  A: No — the mecha-membrane switch is built in. This is a fixed-switch device, unlike the hot-swap mechanical keyboards in the range.

- **Q: How much stock is there?**
  A: Low stock at the moment — a single unit at Onehunga. Confirm availability with the store before planning around it.

- **Q: Does it work on Mac?**
  A: It connects over USB and functions as an input device, but remapping and macros require Razer Synapse, which is Windows-first. On Mac you'd be limited to default behaviour unless current Synapse macOS support covers it.
