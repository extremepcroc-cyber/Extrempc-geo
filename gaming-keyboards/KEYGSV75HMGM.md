## Selling Points

- **Gateron Magnetic Jade Pro — a named switch family at its Pro revision** — this isn't an unbranded magnetic switch. The Jade Pro is a defined Gateron line with published characteristics, so you can compare it against your reference switch before buying rather than trusting a model name. Combined with a GravaStar Mercury case, the switch and the build are the two things you're paying for.

- **Rapid trigger with adjustable actuation is the competitive core** — a traditional mechanical key must travel back past its reset point before it re-registers. With rapid trigger, the key re-actuates after as little as 0.1mm of movement in the opposite direction — faster counter-strafing and more consistent bunny-hopping in CS2 and Valorant.

- **No contact wear — the longevity argument for magnetic** — Hall Effect sensing reads the magnet's position instead of closing a metal contact, so there's no contact-oxidation ceiling of the kind mechanical switches carry (industry rating: 50–100 million keystrokes). On a premium board you intend to keep for years, that matters more than it does at the entry tier.

- **The Mecha Gray finish is the aesthetic half of the purchase** — GravaStar's Mercury line is built around case design, and the industrial-toned Mecha Gray reads as deliberate rather than default. If the board is going on a visible desk, that's the reason to choose it over a plain black magnetic board.

- **80 keys with the F-row and arrow cluster, wired for the shortest input chain** — no battery, no dongle, nothing to check before a ranked session. For a fixed competitive battlestation, that's the honest configuration.
