## Selling Points

- **Hall Effect magnetic switches in a 84-key TKL — the combination most brands don't offer** — most magnetic boards in the NZ market are 60%–65% compact, which means dropping the F-row and arrow cluster. The G84 HE keeps the full TKL function block with the arrow cluster and media keys while running magnetic switches. If you need both the shortcuts and rapid trigger, this is where those two requirements meet.

- **Creamy Jade HE — the magnetic version of Epomaker's signature switch line** — the Creamy Jade name carries over to the HE variant, so you get the smooth linear character Epomaker is known for, but with magnetic sensing instead of contact-based actuation. Magnetic switches have no metal contacts to oxidise, so there's no 50–100 million keystroke wear ceiling.

- **Rapid trigger is the reason to pay the magnetic premium** — with a traditional mechanical switch, the key must travel back past its reset point before it can register again. With rapid trigger, the key re-actuates after as little as 0.1mm of movement in the opposite direction. In CS2 and Valorant that shows up as faster counter-strafing and consistent bunny-hopping.

- **Tri-mode wireless on a magnetic board** — 2.4GHz for low-latency gaming, Bluetooth for multi-device pairing, and USB-C wired. Many magnetic boards are wired-only; the G84 HE runs wireless without giving up the magnetic feature set.

- **Hot-swappable despite the magnetic platform** — 5-pin/3-pin sockets mean the switch choice isn't locked. If you later want a different magnetic weight or a mechanical switch, the platform accommodates it.

## FAQ

- **Q: Does it actually support rapid trigger?**
  A: The Epomaker HE series spec includes software-adjustable actuation and rapid trigger. If rapid trigger is a hard requirement for your play, confirm the current firmware feature set with the Build Team before purchase — they can verify against the live spec sheet at Onehunga.

- **Q: How is this different from a mechanical keyboard in day-to-day use?**
  A: Two differences you'll notice. First, the actuation point is adjustable in software rather than fixed by the switch, so you can set the key to trigger earlier or later. Second, HE switches typically need a heavier return spring to overcome the magnet-sensor interaction, so a "light" HE switch can feel heavier on the return than a comparably-rated mechanical linear. For gaming the faster reset is the payoff; for long typing sessions it's worth trying first.

- **Q: Is it really tri-mode, or just 2.4GHz?**
  A: Tri-mode. 2.4GHz via dongle for low-latency gaming, Bluetooth for multi-device pairing, and USB-C wired. Switch modes on the board or via the companion software.
