## Selling Points

- **Apollo magnetic switches in a full TKL layout** — the Mix 87 keeps the F-row and the arrow cluster while running magnetic switches, which most boards in this category trade away for a compact footprint. If you need the shortcuts and rapid trigger together, this is where the two meet.

- **Black is the colourway that doesn't ask for attention** — against a black desk, black case, and dark monitor bezel, the board disappears into the setup and the per-key RGB becomes the only colour. For a build where the screen is the focus, that's the right choice.

- **Rapid trigger is the competitive feature worth the step up from a mechanical board** — with rapid trigger, a key re-actuates after as little as 0.1mm of movement in the opposite direction rather than requiring a full return past a fixed reset point. In CS2 and Valorant that's faster counter-strafing and more consistent bunny-hopping.

- **Magnetic switches have no contact wear** — Hall Effect sensing reads the magnet's position instead of closing a metal contact, so there's no contact-oxidation ceiling of the kind mechanical switches carry (industry rating: 50–100 million keystrokes).

- **Wired USB-C — the shortest possible input chain** — no battery, no dongle, no charging routine. On a fixed competitive battlestation, that's the configuration that removes variables.

## FAQ

- **Q: Does it support rapid trigger?**
  A: MCHOSE's magnetic-switch keyboards support adjustable actuation and rapid trigger. If rapid trigger is a hard requirement, confirm the current firmware feature set with the Build Team before purchase — they can verify at Onehunga.

- **Q: What's the difference between the Apollo and Kailh magnetic switches in MCHOSE's range?**
  A: Different vendors with different tuning. Apollo (Mix 87) and Kailh Magnetic God (Jet 75) are separate switch lines — the practical difference is spring weight and sound signature. Both support the magnetic feature set (adjustable actuation, rapid trigger). Test both in store if the feel matters.

- **Q: Why is it wired only?**
  A: At this tier, wired keeps the price down and removes the battery and dongle variables. If you need wireless magnetic, Epomaker's G84 HE is the tri-mode option.

- **Q: Is it good for typing as well as gaming?**
  A: Yes, with one caveat: HE switches typically need a heavier return spring to overcome the magnet-sensor interaction, so a "light" HE switch can feel heavier on the return than a comparably-rated mechanical linear. For long typing sessions, try it in store first.

- **Q: Can I use it with a Mac?**
  A: USB-C wired works on macOS out of the box. The actuation and rapid-trigger software is Windows-first; on Mac you'll use the system defaults.
