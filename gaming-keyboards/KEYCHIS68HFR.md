## Selling Points

- **WS Flux magnetic switches with rapid trigger and adjustable actuation** — the Slice68 HE's core feature set is the same competitive capability that Wooting pioneered: actuation you can tune in software, and a key that re-actuates after a small movement in the opposite direction rather than requiring a full return past a fixed reset point. For CS2 and Valorant that's faster counter-strafing and more consistent bunny-hopping.

- **65% layout — the smallest footprint that keeps a normal typing surface** — 68 keys keeps the letter block and modifiers and puts the F-row and arrows on the Fn layer, freeing desk width for the mouse. It's the layout for a desk where flicks need room, or a small setup where a bigger board would crowd everything.

- **Red is the colourway the other magnetic boards don't offer at this tier** — the magnetic segment runs heavily black and white. A red case is a deliberate aesthetic choice rather than a default, and it suits red-accented builds and warm-toned RGB themes.

- **Magnetic switches have no contact wear** — Hall Effect sensing reads the magnet's position instead of closing a metal contact, so there's no contact-oxidation ceiling of the kind mechanical switches carry (industry rating: 50–100 million keystrokes).

- **Hot-swappable sockets despite the magnetic platform** — the switch choice isn't locked to the WS Flux. The sockets accept the standard families, so the board survives a change of preference.
