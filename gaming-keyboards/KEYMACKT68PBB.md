## Selling Points

- **The smart screen is the reason this board exists in the range** — an integrated display showing status and customisable content. At this price point most boards are a case, switches, and keycaps; a screen is a feature you'd normally find a tier or more up. If you want a keyboard that does something visibly different, this is it.

- **Brown tactile switches — the middle path between red and blue** — a brown switch has a tactile bump you can feel at the actuation point but no audible click. It's the switch for people who want typing confirmation without the noise of a clicky blue, which makes it the most generally usable mechanical type for mixed work and gaming.

- **Wireless with a wired fallback** — 2.4GHz for low-latency gaming plus USB-C when you want zero interference or need to charge. At this price tier, wireless is a real step up over the wired-only boards in the range.

- **65% layout with a small footprint** — 68 keys frees the desk width a full-size board commits, leaving more room for mouse movement. The F-row and arrows live on the Fn layer.

- **Hot-swap sockets** — the brown tactile isn't a permanent commitment. The sockets accept the standard switch families, so you can move to a linear or a clicky later without soldering.

## FAQ

- **Q: What does the smart screen show?**
  A: The KT68 Pro's integrated screen displays status information and customisable content. To see exactly what's available on the current firmware — and how you configure it — ask the Build Team to demonstrate on a unit at Onehunga.

- **Q: Is it really wireless?**
  A: Yes — 2.4GHz wireless plus USB-C wired. Note the BC listing identifies it as a wireless mechanical keyboard and the screen generally confirms the active mode.

- **Q: What are brown switches — are they good for gaming?**
  A: Brown is a tactile mechanical switch: a bump you can feel at the actuation point with no audible click. It's a good all-rounder — enough tactility for typing confirmation, light enough for gaming, and quiet enough for a shared space. If you want maximum speed, red linears; if you want maximum feedback, a clicky.

- **Q: Are the switches hot-swappable?**
  A: Yes. The sockets accept the standard switch families, so the brown tactiles can be swapped for linears or clickies later without soldering.

- **Q: How much stock is there?**
  A: Low stock at the moment — a few units at Onehunga. Confirm availability with the store before planning around it.

- **Q: Can I use it with a Mac?**
  A: USB-C wired works on macOS out of the box, and the 2.4GHz dongle also works on Mac. Key remapping software is Windows-first; on Mac you'll use the system defaults.
