## Selling Points

- **Apollo magnetic switches in a TKL — full function block plus rapid trigger** — MCHOSE sources magnetic switches from several vendors, and the Mix 87 carries Apollo. The TKL layout keeps the F-row and the arrow cluster, which most magnetic boards drop for a compact footprint. If you need both the shortcuts and rapid trigger, this is where those meet.

- **Rose red is genuinely rare on a gaming keyboard** — the market is overwhelmingly black, white, and grey. A rose red case is a deliberate choice for a setup that isn't trying to look like every other one, and it photographs distinctly rather than blending into a wall of black peripherals.

- **Rapid trigger is the competitive feature worth paying for** — a traditional mechanical switch must travel back past its reset point before it can re-register. With rapid trigger, the key re-actuates after as little as 0.1mm of movement in the opposite direction. In CS2 and Valorant that shows up as faster counter-strafing and more consistent bunny-hopping.

- **Magnetic switches have no contact wear** — Hall Effect sensing reads the magnet's position instead of closing a metal contact, so there's no contact-oxidation ceiling of the kind mechanical switches carry (industry rating: 50–100 million keystrokes).

- **Wired USB-C — no battery, no dongle, no charging routine** — at this tier, wired keeps the input chain as short as possible. For a fixed competitive battlestation, that's the honest configuration.

## FAQ

- **Q: Does it support rapid trigger?**
  A: MCHOSE's magnetic-switch keyboards support adjustable actuation and rapid trigger. If rapid trigger is a hard requirement, confirm the current firmware feature set with the Build Team before purchase — they can verify against the live spec sheet at Onehunga.

- **Q: Is the Apollo switch made by MCHOSE?**
  A: No — Apollo is one of the third-party magnetic switch vendors MCHOSE uses, alongside Kailh (Jet 75) and others. Because the board is hot-swap, you can change the switch later if you prefer a different feel.

- **Q: Why is it wired only?**
  A: At this tier, wired keeps the price down and removes the battery and dongle variables. If you need wireless magnetic, the Epomaker G84 HE is the tri-mode option at a higher tier.

- **Q: How does the magnetic feel differ from a mechanical switch?**
  A: Two things you'll notice. The actuation point is adjustable in software rather than fixed by the switch. And HE switches typically need a heavier return spring to overcome the magnet-sensor interaction, so a "light" HE switch can feel heavier on the return than a comparably-rated mechanical linear.

- **Q: Can I use it with a Mac?**
  A: USB-C wired works on macOS out of the box. The actuation and rapid-trigger software is Windows-first; on Mac you'll use the system defaults.
