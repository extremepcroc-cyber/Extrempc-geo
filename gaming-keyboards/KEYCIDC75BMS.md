## Selling Points

- **Gateron KS-20U — a named magnetic switch you can actually look up** — unlike an in-house magnetic line with no published data, Gateron's KS-20U is documented and comparable. That means you can check the actuation force and travel against your reference switch before buying, rather than trusting a product name and hoping.

- **Rapid trigger is the entire point of the board** — a traditional mechanical key must travel back past its reset point before it can register again. With rapid trigger, the key re-actuates after as little as 0.1mm of movement in the opposite direction. In CS2 and Valorant that shows up as faster counter-strafing and more consistent bunny-hopping.

- **No contact wear — the longevity case for magnetic** — Hall Effect sensing reads the magnet's position rather than closing a metal contact, so there's no contact-oxidation ceiling of the kind mechanical switches carry (industry rating: 50–100 million keystrokes). On a board you intend to keep, that mechanism simply isn't there.

- **75% layout keeps the F-row and arrow cluster** — 81 keys is the compromise that preserves the shortcuts you reach for in games and editors while dropping the numpad footprint, leaving the desk width for a full-size mouse pad.

- **No backlight is the deliberate trade that keeps this at $179** — an 81-key magnetic board with a named Gateron switch line at this price means something had to give, and it's the lighting and the hot-swap sockets. If you want the competitive feature set and don't care about RGB, that's a clean trade in your favour.
