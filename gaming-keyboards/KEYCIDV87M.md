## FAQ

- **Q: What is VIA, and do I need to code anything?**
  A: VIA is a browser-based configurator with a visual keymap editor — click a key, choose its function, save. No code required for remapping, layers, or macros. It also handles the knob's assignment. QMK is the deeper code-level firmware underneath, and most users never touch it.

- **Q: What can the knob be assigned to?**
  A: By default it's a volume dial. Via VIA you can assign it to other functions — scrolling, zooming, layer switching. The exact assignment list depends on the current VIA build, and the team can show you the options on a unit at Onehunga.

- **Q: Does it have Bluetooth?**
  A: No — 2.4GHz wireless plus USB-C wired. For Bluetooth multi-device pairing, the AULA and MCHOSE tri-mode boards in the range are the alternatives.

- **Q: Are the switches hot-swappable?**
  A: Yes. The sockets accept the standard switch families, so the Matte switches can be replaced without soldering — one of the two things this board keeps that the cheaper CIDOO models drop.

- **Q: How much stock is there?**
  A: One unit on the shelf at Onehunga at the moment. A phone call confirms whether it's still there and when the next shipment lands.

- **Q: Can I use it with a Mac?**
  A: The 2.4GHz receiver and USB-C wired work on macOS, and VIA runs in a Chromium-based browser, so it's available on Mac too. That's an advantage over boards whose remapping utility is Windows-only.
