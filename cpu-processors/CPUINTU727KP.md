## Comparison

- **vs Intel Core Ultra 5 250K Plus (CPUINT5250KO, 18-core LGA1851)** — 250K Plus has 18 cores (8P + 8E + 2LP). The 270K Plus adds 6 more E-cores (12 vs 8) for heavier multi-thread workloads. Worth the step for content-creation and heavy multitask; for gaming the gap is small.

- **vs Intel Core Ultra 7 265K (CPUINT7265KOEM, 20-core LGA1851)** — 265K has 8P + 12E (20 cores total). The 270K Plus adds 4 LP E-cores for the always-on low-power tier. Worth the step if the LP E-cores matter for your workload; for pure gaming the gap is small.

- **vs Intel Core Ultra 9 285K (CPUINT9285KOEM, 24-core flagship LGA1851)** — 285K is the flagship with higher boost clocks and the top of the Arrow Lake tier. Worth the step for the absolute fastest single-thread and max multi-thread on LGA1851; for most builds the 270K Plus is sufficient.

- **vs AMD Ryzen 9 9900X (CPUAMD9900XOEM, 12-core AM5 Zen 5)** — Different platform. AMD 9900X has fewer cores (12 vs 24) but a more efficient envelope (170W vs the 270K Plus's 125W+) and a longer platform commitment (AM5 through 2027+). For raw multi-thread throughput, the 270K Plus wins; for efficiency and platform longevity, the 9900X is the stronger buy.

- **vs AMD Ryzen 9 9950X (CPUAMDR99950XOEM, 16-core AM5 Zen 5)** — Same logic: the 270K Plus wins raw core count (24 vs 16); the 9950X wins efficiency and platform longevity. Pick by platform preference and existing hardware.

## FAQ

- **Q: Does the 270K Plus come with a cooler?**
  A: No. The OEM tray packaging does not include a stock cooler. Pair with a quality tower air (Thermalright Peerless Assassin 120 SE, be quiet! Dark Rock Pro 5) or a 240mm/280mm AIO for the 125W+ max turbo envelope.

- **Q: Does LGA1851 support DDR4?**
  A: No. LGA1851 is DDR5-only. A DDR5 kit is required. If you have an existing DDR4 board, it's LGA1700 and won't fit this CPU.

- **Q: What's the difference between the 270K Plus and the 265K?**
  A: Both are LGA1851 Arrow Lake 24-core-class parts. The 265K is 8P + 12E (20 cores). The 270K Plus adds 4 LP E-cores for the always-on low-power tier. For most builds the difference is small; for 24/7 always-on workloads the LP E-cores matter.

- **Q: Is the Arc iGPU good enough for gaming?**
  A: For light 1080p gaming and office / media use, yes. For modern AAA at high settings, a discrete GPU is required. The Arc iGPU is the integrated-graphics tier, not a replacement for a discrete card.

- **Q: What cooler do I need for the 270K Plus?**
  A: A quality tower air (Thermalright Peerless Assassin 120 SE, be quiet! Dark Rock Pro 5) or a 240mm/280mm AIO. The 125W+ max turbo envelope is manageable with good airflow.
