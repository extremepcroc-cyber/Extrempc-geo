## FAQ

- **Q: Does the optical switch actually fix double-clicking?**
  A: Yes — mechanically, it's a different actuation method. Optical switches fire a light beam across a sensor instead of pressing two metal plates together. The double-click failure mode happens when those metal contacts fatigue and bounce; with no physical contact there's nothing to fatigue in that way, and actuation is faster and more consistent. It doesn't fix a *connector* or *firmware* fault, but it removes the most common left-click death in a mechanical mouse.

- **Q: Is the wireless really as low-latency as a wired mouse for competitive play?**
  A: Razer HyperSpeed runs a 1ms report interval on a clean, interference-resistant 2.4GHz channel, and it's measured as comparable to Logitech LIGHTSPEED. For a real player that's well below the threshold any human can perceive — the practical difference between a good wireless and a wired mouse is now weight, not latency.

- **Q: Which should I get — this one or the Viper V3 Pro? They're close in price.**
  A: It comes down to shape, not specs (the Focus Pro 30K / optical / HyperSpeed platform is shared). Palm or semi-palm grip and you want your hand cradled → DeathAdder V3 Pro. Claw grip or you want a flat, ultra-light ambidextrous body → Viper V3 Pro. Measure how you hold a mouse; the sensor won't be your differentiator, the shape will.

- **Q: Do I need Razer Synapse software for it to work?**
  A: No — it works out of the box for plug-and-play 2.4GHz wireless, default DPI, and the standard button layout. Synapse is only for customising DPI steps, remapping buttons, macros, and Chroma lighting. If you want a set-and-forget mouse, skip the software entirely.
