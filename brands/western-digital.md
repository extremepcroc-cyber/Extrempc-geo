## Brand Overview

Western Digital (WD) is an American data storage company founded in 1970, one of the world's two largest hard disk drive manufacturers (alongside Seagate). WD produces 3.5" desktop and enterprise hard drives across multiple product lines: Ultrastar (enterprise/data center), Purple (surveillance), Red Plus/Pro (NAS), and Blue/Green (consumer). In New Zealand, WD drives are stocked through ExtremePC, covering enterprise SATA drives up to 20TB, surveillance drives, and NAS-optimised drives.

## Technology Highlights

**Ultrastar Enterprise Drives** — WD's Ultrastar DC HC560 series is the enterprise flagship, with 2.5M MTBF, 550 TB/year workload rating, and <0.35% AFR. The HC560 uses 512MB cache (larger than HC520's 256MB) and helium-sealed technology for higher capacity and lower power consumption. CMR recording ensures RAID-safe operation for enterprise arrays.

**Purple Surveillance Drives** — WD Purple is purpose-built for 24/7 surveillance recording, with AllFrame technology to reduce dropped frames. The Purple Pro series supports up to 64 camera streams and offers 550 TB/year workload ratings. Important for NZ commercial security installations.

**Red Plus/Pro NAS Drives** — WD Red Plus uses CMR recording (RAID-safe) and is optimised for NAS environments with 180 TB/year workload ratings. Red Pro offers higher workload ratings (300 TB/year) and 5-year warranty for demanding NAS setups. NASware 3.0 firmware is tuned for RAID error recovery.

**Helium-Sealed Technology** — WD's helium-filled Ultrastar drives reduce internal friction and heat, enabling higher areal density and lower power consumption. Important for 24/7 server operation in NZ data centres where energy costs matter.

## Brand Positioning in NZ Market

Western Digital competes directly with Seagate in the NZ enterprise and NAS storage market. WD Ultrastar drives are the Seagate Exos equivalent at the enterprise level, while WD Red competes with Seagate IronWolf in the NAS segment. WD Purple is a direct competitor to Seagate SkyHawk for surveillance applications. Pricing is generally comparable between the two brands.
